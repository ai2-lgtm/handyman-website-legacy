Navigating Tenant Maintenance Rights in Dubai

By Handyman.ae Team · Updated May 2026 · Expert Guide

Renting a villa in Arabian Ranches or an apartment in Dubai Marina comes with its unique set of maintenance challenges, especially with the relentless 45°C+ summers and high humidity. As a tenant, understanding your rights and responsibilities regarding property upkeep is crucial to avoid disputes and ensure a comfortable living environment. This guide, from Dubai's handyman., cuts through the complexities of Ejari and RERA regulations, offering clear, actionable insights on who handles what when it comes to property maintenance in the emirate.

Understanding Your Tenancy Contract and Ejari

The foundation of any tenant-landlord relationship in Dubai is the tenancy contract, which must be registered with Ejari. This registration is not merely a formality; it legally binds both parties and outlines the terms, including maintenance clauses. Typically, contracts differentiate between major (structural) and minor (day-to-day) maintenance. While the general rule of thumb often places major repairs on the landlord and minor ones on the tenant, the specifics can vary. For instance, a common clause might state that the tenant is responsible for maintenance up to a certain cost threshold, say, AED 500 (though we avoid specific figures, this is a common contractual structure). It's imperative to read these clauses thoroughly before signing. Ejari registration ensures your contract is recognised by the Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) should any disagreements arise. Without it, your legal standing is significantly weakened. Understanding the nuances of your Ejari-registered contract is the first step in asserting your maintenance rights and fulfilling your obligations, especially when dealing with issues exacerbated by Dubai's climate, such as AC performance or plumbing integrity.

Landlord's Core Maintenance Obligations

In Dubai, landlords are generally responsible for major structural repairs and ensuring the property remains habitable. This includes the integrity of the building's structure, major plumbing systems, electrical wiring, and the functionality of essential appliances provided with the property, such as a built-in oven or a Daikin AC unit. For example, if the main AC compressor fails in a villa in Damac Hills, preventing proper cooling during a 48°C summer day, this falls squarely under the landlord's responsibility. Similarly, issues with the main water heater (e.g., Ariston or Ferroli) or significant leaks from the roof or main pipes are typically the landlord's concern. They must ensure these systems are in good working order at the start of the tenancy and address any major breakdowns promptly. Failure to do so can be grounds for a formal complaint to the RERA Rental Disputes Centre. A tradesman inspecting a major AC fault would check refrigerant levels, compressor health, and main electrical connections, confirming it's beyond routine tenant upkeep.

Tenant's Day-to-Day Maintenance Responsibilities

Tenants are typically responsible for minor, routine maintenance that arises from daily use. This includes tasks like replacing light bulbs, clearing minor drain blockages, maintaining garden areas (if applicable), and ensuring general cleanliness. In Dubai's environment, this also extends to regular cleaning of AC filters to maintain efficiency and air quality, especially given the pervasive sand intrusion. Neglecting filter cleaning can lead to reduced cooling efficiency and higher DEWA bills. For properties with district cooling (Empower/Tabreed), tenants are usually responsible for the fan coil unit's internal maintenance, including filter changes. Small plumbing issues, such as a dripping tap or a running toilet, are also often the tenant's purview. It's about maintaining the property in the condition it was received, barring fair wear and tear. A proactive approach to these minor tasks can prevent them from escalating into larger, more costly problems, which might then become a point of contention.

Addressing Urgent Repairs and Emergencies

Urgent repairs, such as a burst pipe, a complete power outage, or a non-functional AC during peak summer, require immediate attention. In such scenarios, the tenant should notify the landlord or property management immediately. While the landlord is responsible for rectifying these, the tenant's role is to prevent further damage. For instance, in the event of a burst pipe, turning off the main water supply is a critical first step. The tenancy contract should ideally outline the procedure for emergency repairs, including response times. If the landlord fails to act within a reasonable timeframe for an emergency that renders the property uninhabitable, the tenant may have grounds to undertake the repair and seek reimbursement, though this should be done with caution and proper documentation. Dubai's extreme climate means AC failures are often considered emergencies, as indoor humidity above 60% can invite mold within 48 hours on cool surfaces, posing health risks.

Preventative Maintenance in Dubai's Climate

Given Dubai's harsh climate, preventative maintenance is not just advisable but essential for both parties. For tenants, this means regularly cleaning AC filters, ensuring proper ventilation to combat 90%+ humidity, and promptly reporting any small issues before they become major. For landlords, it involves scheduling annual AC servicing, pest control, and checking for salt air corrosion on external units in areas like Palm Jumeirah or JBR. A professional AC service, for example, would check the supply temperature drop across the coil, which should ideally be 8-11°C, indicating efficient cooling. They would also inspect for sand intrusion in outdoor units and ensure proper drainage to prevent water damage. Proactive measures significantly extend the lifespan of appliances and systems, reducing the likelihood of costly breakdowns and tenant inconvenience. This shared responsibility in preventative care fosters a better living environment and minimises disputes over wear and tear.

Dispute Resolution: RERA and Rental Disputes Centre

Should a maintenance dispute escalate beyond direct communication, the RERA Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) is the official body for resolution in Dubai. Before approaching the RDC, tenants should ensure they have thoroughly documented all communications with the landlord, including dates, times, and details of the issues reported and actions taken (or not taken). Photographic or video evidence of the maintenance issue is also crucial. The RDC offers mediation services and, if necessary, formal hearings to resolve disputes based on the Ejari-registered contract and RERA laws. It's a formal process, so having all documentation in order is vital. Understanding the RDC's procedures and your rights under RERA can empower tenants to seek fair resolution when landlords fail to meet their maintenance obligations, or when there's disagreement over who is responsible for a particular repair.

Navigating End-of-Tenancy Maintenance

The end of a tenancy often brings maintenance discussions to the forefront, particularly regarding the return of the security deposit. Landlords expect the property to be returned in a condition similar to how it was received, accounting for fair wear and tear. This typically means tenants are responsible for professional deep cleaning, minor touch-up painting, and ensuring all minor repairs (e.g., fixing small holes from picture frames, replacing broken light fixtures) are addressed. Issues like significant wall damage, major appliance breakdowns not due to tenant misuse, or structural problems remain the landlord's responsibility. It's advisable for both parties to conduct a thorough move-out inspection, ideally comparing it against a move-in condition report or inventory. Documenting the property's condition with photos or videos at both stages can prevent disputes over the security deposit. Understanding these expectations upfront can save considerable stress and financial implications at the end of your lease in areas like JVC or Downtown Dubai.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is responsible for AC maintenance in a Dubai rental property?

Generally, landlords handle major AC repairs, while tenants are responsible for routine tasks like cleaning filters. The tenancy contract, especially for district cooling systems, specifies these details.

Can a tenant undertake repairs if the landlord fails to act?

In emergencies where the landlord is unresponsive, a tenant may undertake repairs to prevent further damage, but this should be done with proper documentation and intent to seek reimbursement.

What is considered 'fair wear and tear' in Dubai tenancy?

Fair wear and tear refers to the natural deterioration of a property from ordinary use. It does not include damage caused by negligence or misuse, which tenants are responsible for.

How does Ejari affect maintenance disputes?

An Ejari-registered contract provides legal standing for both parties in maintenance disputes, allowing the RERA Rental Disputes Centre to intervene and resolve issues based on contractual terms.

Are landlords responsible for pest control in Dubai?

Typically, landlords are responsible for initial pest control before tenancy. During tenancy, it often falls to the tenant unless the infestation is due to structural issues or a widespread building problem.

What should I do if my landlord refuses to carry out a major repair?

Document all communications, gather evidence of the issue, and if direct resolution fails, file a complaint with the RERA Rental Disputes Centre for mediation and legal resolution.
