Navigating Landlord Maintenance Obligations in Dubai Properties

By Handyman.ae Team · Updated May 2026 · Expert Guide

Dubai's unique climate and regulatory landscape present distinct challenges and responsibilities for landlords regarding property maintenance. With summer temperatures regularly exceeding 45°C and humidity levels often above 90%, proactive maintenance is not merely a convenience but a necessity to preserve property integrity and tenant comfort. From the sand intrusion common in areas like Arabian Ranches and Damac Hills to the corrosive salt air affecting properties in Palm Jumeirah and JBR, understanding the precise demarcation of maintenance duties between landlord and tenant is crucial. This guide, from Dubai's handyman., delves into the specifics of RERA and Ejari regulations, common maintenance pitfalls in high-rise Marina towers or sprawling JVC communities, and practical advice for ensuring properties remain in optimal condition amidst Dubai's demanding environment.

Understanding RERA and Ejari Frameworks

Dubai’s real estate landscape is governed by specific regulations that delineate landlord and tenant responsibilities, primarily through the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) and the Ejari registration system. RERA Law No. 26 of 2007, concerning the relationship between landlords and tenants, forms the bedrock. It stipulates that the landlord is generally responsible for major maintenance and structural repairs, ensuring the property remains fit for habitation. This includes rectifying defects that affect the fundamental usability or safety of the property. Ejari, the mandatory registration of tenancy contracts, formalises the agreement and provides a legal framework for dispute resolution. A properly registered Ejari contract is essential for both parties, as it outlines the agreed-upon terms, including maintenance clauses. Without clear documentation, disputes can escalate, particularly in a transient market like Dubai. For instance, if a major appliance, such as a built-in Bosch oven in a Downtown apartment, fails due to age or manufacturing defect, the landlord typically bears the replacement cost. Conversely, minor repairs resulting from tenant misuse or neglect, like a broken light switch, fall under tenant responsibility. Understanding these foundational elements is critical before delving into specific maintenance categories. The RERA framework aims to protect both parties, ensuring properties are maintained to a reasonable standard while preventing tenants from incurring undue costs for wear and tear beyond their control. This legal clarity is particularly vital in high-value areas like Palm Jumeirah, where property upkeep directly impacts asset value and tenant satisfaction.

Structural Integrity and Major Repairs

Maintaining the structural integrity of a property is unequivocally the landlord’s responsibility in Dubai. This encompasses the building’s foundation, walls, roof, and any load-bearing elements. Issues such as significant cracks in walls, water seepage from the roof, or foundational shifts, though rare, demand immediate attention from the landlord. These are not cosmetic fixes but fundamental repairs that ensure the safety and longevity of the property. For villas in Arabian Ranches or Dubai Hills Estate, this might involve addressing issues with external rendering due to extreme temperature fluctuations or ensuring proper drainage to prevent water accumulation around the foundation. In high-rise apartments in Marina or Business Bay, structural concerns could relate to facade integrity or shared building systems. Landlords are also responsible for major fixtures and fittings that are integral to the property’s function, such as built-in wardrobes, kitchen cabinets, and essential sanitary ware. For example, if a shower tray in a JBR apartment develops a leak due to faulty installation or material degradation, the repair or replacement falls to the landlord. This also extends to the common areas of a building, where the Owners Association, often managed by the developer, is responsible for the upkeep, but the landlord, as the owner, is ultimately accountable for ensuring these common areas do not negatively impact their tenant’s unit. Any major repair that impacts the habitability or safety of the property, and is not a result of tenant misuse, must be addressed by the landlord promptly to comply with RERA guidelines.

AC Systems: A Dubai Essential

Given Dubai’s relentless heat, a functional air conditioning system is paramount, making its maintenance a frequent point of discussion. Generally, landlords are responsible for the major repairs and replacement of AC units if they fail due to age, manufacturing defects, or normal wear and tear. This applies to central AC systems in villas in areas like Arabian Ranches or individual split units in apartments in JVC. Tenants, however, are typically responsible for routine maintenance, such as filter cleaning and ensuring proper usage. A common issue in Dubai is the accumulation of dust and sand, which can severely impact AC efficiency. For instance, if a Daikin or Carrier unit in a Dubai Hills villa stops cooling effectively, a qualified technician will first check the refrigerant levels and the compressor. If the compressor has failed due to normal operational lifespan, the landlord is responsible for its replacement. However, if the issue stems from clogged filters due to lack of cleaning, the tenant would bear the cost of cleaning and any subsequent repairs caused by this neglect. It’s crucial to note that in district cooling areas like Business Bay or Downtown, where Empower or Tabreed supply chilled water, the landlord is responsible for the fan coil units (FCUs) within the property, while the district cooling provider manages the central plant. A key diagnostic for AC performance is measuring the temperature drop across the coil; a healthy system should show an 8-11°C drop. Anything less indicates a problem, often requiring professional intervention that, if not tenant-induced, falls to the landlord.

Plumbing and Water Systems

Functional plumbing and water systems are fundamental to any habitable property, and in Dubai, these systems face unique challenges. Landlords are responsible for the integrity of the main water supply lines, drainage systems, and water heaters. Issues such as burst pipes, major leaks from concealed plumbing, or a complete failure of an Ariston or Ferroli water heater are typically the landlord’s burden. The high mineral content in Dubai’s water can lead to scale buildup, affecting water heater efficiency and pipe longevity. Regular descaling, while often a tenant responsibility for minor appliances, becomes a landlord’s concern for integrated systems. For instance, if a main drain line in a JVC apartment becomes blocked due to structural issues or tree root intrusion (more common in older villa communities), the landlord must arrange for its clearing and repair. However, blockages caused by tenant misuse, such as flushing inappropriate items down toilets, are the tenant’s responsibility. The salt air in coastal areas like Palm Jumeirah and JBR can also accelerate corrosion of external plumbing fixtures, requiring landlords to invest in more durable materials or more frequent replacements. Water pressure issues, if not related to municipal supply, also fall under the landlord’s purview, as they indicate potential problems with the building’s internal pumping systems. Ensuring a reliable and clean water supply, free from major leaks, is a core landlord obligation, directly impacting tenant health and property value.

Electrical Systems and Safety

Electrical systems are critical for safety and functionality, and landlords in Dubai bear the primary responsibility for their safe operation and maintenance. This includes the main distribution board, wiring, sockets, and light fixtures. Any major electrical fault, such as frequent tripping of the main breaker, exposed wiring, or issues with the property’s earthing system, must be addressed by the landlord. DEWA (Dubai Electricity and Water Authority) regulations are stringent, and landlords must ensure their properties comply with all safety standards. For example, if a power surge damages the main electrical panel in a Dubai Marina apartment, the landlord is responsible for its repair or replacement. However, tenants are responsible for replacing light bulbs and fuses for minor appliances, and for ensuring their own electrical devices are safe and not overloading circuits. In older properties, especially villas in areas like Al Barsha or Al Quoz, landlords might need to upgrade outdated wiring to meet current safety codes. Issues arising from wear and tear, such as a faulty wall socket that no longer holds a plug securely, are also typically the landlord’s concern. It’s important for landlords to conduct periodic electrical inspections to prevent hazards, especially considering the high demand placed on AC units during the summer, which can strain electrical infrastructure. A professional electrician will check for loose connections, correct voltage, and proper insulation, ensuring the system can safely handle the load. Any defect that poses a risk of electric shock or fire is a landlord’s urgent responsibility.

Pest Control and Environmental Factors

Dubai’s climate, with its high temperatures and humidity, creates an environment conducive to various pests, making pest control a significant maintenance consideration. Generally, landlords are responsible for initial pest control treatments to ensure the property is delivered free of infestations. If a severe infestation of common Dubai pests like cockroaches, ants, or rodents occurs shortly after a tenant moves in, it is typically the landlord’s responsibility to address it. This is particularly true for properties in ground-floor villas or those adjacent to undeveloped land, where pest ingress is more common. However, ongoing pest control and infestations arising from poor hygiene or tenant-induced conditions (e.g., leaving food exposed) usually fall to the tenant. For instance, if a tenant in a JVC apartment reports a persistent ant problem after several months, the landlord might investigate the source. If it’s a structural issue, like cracks in the building allowing entry, the landlord is responsible. If it’s due to tenant habits, the tenant bears the cost. Furthermore, environmental factors like sand intrusion, prevalent in areas like Damac Hills or Arabian Ranches, require regular cleaning and maintenance. While tenants are responsible for general cleanliness, landlords might need to address structural gaps or faulty seals that allow excessive sand ingress. Mold growth, often a consequence of high humidity (above 60% indoors invites mold within 48 hours on cool surfaces) and poor ventilation, can be a shared responsibility. If mold results from a landlord’s failure to repair a leak or provide adequate ventilation, it’s their duty. If it’s due to tenant behaviour, like not airing out bathrooms, it’s the tenant’s. Proactive measures, such as ensuring proper window and door seals, are landlord responsibilities that mitigate these environmental challenges.

Tenant Responsibilities and Preventative Measures

While landlords bear significant maintenance responsibilities, tenants also play a crucial role in preserving the property’s condition and preventing minor issues from escalating. Tenants are generally responsible for routine upkeep, cleanliness, and minor repairs that arise from their daily use or negligence. This includes tasks such as regularly cleaning AC filters, which can significantly impact efficiency and air quality, especially during the dusty summer months. Replacing light bulbs, fuses, and ensuring proper disposal of waste to prevent drain blockages are also tenant duties. For example, if a tenant in a Business Bay apartment repeatedly flushes non-biodegradable items, leading to a blocked toilet, the cost of plumbing services would fall to them. Tenants are also expected to use appliances and fixtures responsibly. If a Samsung washing machine or a Yale door lock is damaged due to misuse, the tenant is liable for repair or replacement. Preventative measures are key in Dubai’s harsh climate. Tenants should report any maintenance issues promptly to the landlord or property management. Ignoring a small leak, for instance, can lead to extensive water damage and mold growth, which then becomes a more complex and costly repair. Maintaining good ventilation, especially in bathrooms and kitchens, helps combat humidity and reduces the risk of mold. In villas, tenants are typically responsible for garden maintenance unless otherwise specified in the tenancy contract. Understanding and adhering to these responsibilities not only helps maintain the property but also fosters a positive landlord-tenant relationship, reducing potential disputes over maintenance costs.

Dispute Resolution and Communication

Effective communication is the cornerstone of a harmonious landlord-tenant relationship, especially when maintenance issues arise. When a maintenance concern surfaces, the tenant should first communicate it clearly and in writing to the landlord or their appointed property management. This creates a documented record, crucial for any potential dispute resolution. If the landlord fails to address a legitimate maintenance issue within a reasonable timeframe, or if there’s a disagreement over who is responsible, either party can escalate the matter to the Rental Disputes Center (RDC) in Dubai. The RDC, operating under the Dubai Land Department, provides a formal mechanism for resolving landlord-tenant disputes. Before approaching the RDC, it’s advisable to gather all relevant documentation, including the Ejari contract, communication records (emails, WhatsApp messages), and any evidence of the maintenance issue (photos, videos). The RDC encourages mediation as a first step, aiming for an amicable settlement. If mediation fails, the case proceeds to formal hearings. It’s important to understand that the RDC will base its decisions on RERA laws, the Ejari contract, and the evidence presented. For instance, if a tenant in a Palm Jumeirah apartment reports a persistent AC malfunction that the landlord refuses to fix, and the tenant can prove it’s not due to misuse, the RDC would likely rule in the tenant’s favour, compelling the landlord to undertake the repair. Conversely, if a landlord can demonstrate that damage was caused by tenant negligence, the tenant would be held responsible. Clear, documented communication and a thorough understanding of one’s rights and obligations under Dubai law are paramount to navigating maintenance disputes effectively and ensuring a fair outcome for all parties involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is responsible for minor repairs like a leaky tap in Dubai?

A: Minor repairs, often termed 'tenant-induced' or 'wear and tear' issues, are typically the tenant's responsibility. This includes small fixes like a dripping tap or replacing a faulty light switch, unless specified otherwise in the Ejari contract.

Can a landlord charge for AC maintenance in Dubai?

A: Landlords are generally responsible for major AC repairs and replacements. Tenants are usually responsible for routine maintenance like filter cleaning. Specifics should be outlined in the tenancy contract, especially for annual service agreements.

What is the role of Ejari in maintenance disputes?

A: Ejari formalises the tenancy contract, making it legally binding. In maintenance disputes, the Ejari contract serves as the primary legal document outlining agreed-upon responsibilities, which the Rental Disputes Center uses for resolution.

Is pest control the landlord's or tenant's responsibility in Dubai?

A: Landlords are typically responsible for initial pest control to ensure the property is delivered pest-free. Ongoing pest control or infestations due to tenant negligence usually fall to the tenant. Severe, recurring issues may require landlord intervention.

What if a landlord refuses to fix a major issue like a burst pipe?

A: If a landlord neglects a major maintenance issue, the tenant should document all communication and evidence, then escalate the matter to the Rental Disputes Center (RDC) in Dubai for mediation and resolution.

Are landlords responsible for appliance repairs in Dubai?

A: For built-in appliances that are part of the property, such as a built-in oven or water heater, the landlord is generally responsible for major repairs or replacement due to age or defect. Tenant misuse makes the tenant liable.
