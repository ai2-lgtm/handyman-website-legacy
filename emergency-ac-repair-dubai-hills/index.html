The Technical Mechanism Of The Failure

In Dubai Hills Estate, the most common emergency AC failures stem from condensate-line blockages in ducted FCU and VRF systems. These blockages cause water to back up into the attic, triggering float switches that cut power to the system to prevent flooding. Salt humidity and Shamal winds exacerbate corrosion in condensate pipes and electrical connections, leading to shorts and tripped MCBs. The interplay between high summer temperatures (up to 45°C) and system overload further stresses compressors and electronic components.

Another frequent failure mode involves refrigerant leaks detected via BTU sub-meters connected to Empower. Low refrigerant leads to compressor overheating and eventual shutdown. Electrical failures in MCBs or RCDs, often Schneider or ABB branded, result from moisture ingress or aging components.
