Tenant Maintenance Rights in Dubai: What You Need to Know

Renting a property in Dubai comes with a specific set of rules and regulations governed by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA). One of the most common areas of dispute between landlords and tenants is property maintenance. Who pays for the broken AC? Who fixes the leaking pipe? Understanding your tenant maintenance rights in Dubai is crucial for a stress-free tenancy. This guide from Handyman.ae breaks down the responsibilities of both parties.

The General Rule: Major vs. Minor Maintenance

According to Article 16 of Law No. (26) of 2007 (as amended by Law No. 33 of 2008), the landlord is generally responsible for the property's maintenance and for repairing any defects or faults that affect the tenant's intended use of the property, unless agreed otherwise in the tenancy contract.

In practice, the Dubai real estate market operates on a standard division of responsibilities based on a financial threshold, often referred to as "Major" and "Minor" maintenance.

Minor Maintenance (Tenant's Responsibility)

Minor maintenance refers to routine repairs and upkeep resulting from daily wear and tear. The tenancy contract usually specifies a threshold—commonly between AED 500 and AED 1,000 per incident. If the repair cost falls below this threshold, the tenant is responsible for paying it.

Examples of minor maintenance include:

  • Replacing light bulbs and fuses.
  • Unblocking minor drain clogs.
  • Fixing loose cabinet hinges or door handles.
  • Routine AC filter cleaning.
  • Repairing minor leaks in faucets or showerheads.

Major Maintenance (Landlord's Responsibility)

Major maintenance involves significant repairs, structural issues, or the replacement of essential appliances provided with the property. If the repair cost exceeds the agreed-upon threshold, the landlord is responsible for the entire amount (not just the difference).

Examples of major maintenance include:

  • Replacing a faulty AC compressor or motor.
  • Fixing major plumbing leaks or burst pipes inside the walls.
  • Repairing electrical faults in the main distribution board.
  • Fixing structural cracks or severe water damage.
  • Replacing a broken water heater.

The Importance of the Tenancy Contract

While RERA provides the legal framework, the specific terms of your tenancy contract (the addendum attached to the standard Ejari contract) dictate the exact maintenance threshold and any specific clauses. Always read the addendum carefully before signing.

Some landlords may try to include clauses making the tenant responsible for all maintenance, regardless of cost. While you can agree to this, it is highly unusual and generally not recommended for standard residential leases.

Handling Maintenance Disputes

If a major repair is needed and the landlord refuses to fix it, follow these steps:

  1. Document Everything: Take photos and videos of the issue.
  2. Communicate in Writing: Send a formal email to the landlord or property management company detailing the problem and requesting repairs. Keep a record of all correspondence.
  3. Get Professional Quotes: Obtain quotes from licensed maintenance companies like Handyman.ae to prove the cost exceeds the minor maintenance threshold.
  4. File a Complaint: If the landlord still refuses, you can file a case with the Rental Disputes Center (RDC) at the Dubai Land Department. Do not withhold rent, as this is illegal and can lead to eviction.

Move-In and Move-Out Maintenance

Your responsibilities also extend to the beginning and end of your tenancy.

  • Move-In: The landlord must hand over the property in a habitable condition. Document any existing damage on a snagging list within the first few days of moving in to avoid being charged for it later.
  • Move-Out: You are required to return the property in the same condition you received it, minus normal wear and tear. This often involves repainting the walls to the original color and fixing any damage you caused. Read our Move-in/Move-out Handyman Checklist for more details.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Who is responsible for AC maintenance in a rented apartment in Dubai?
Typically, the landlord is responsible for major AC repairs (like compressor replacement), while the tenant is responsible for routine cleaning and minor fixes, unless the tenancy contract states otherwise.
2. What is the standard minor maintenance threshold in Dubai?
The standard threshold for minor maintenance is usually set between AED 500 and AED 1,000 per incident. The tenant pays for repairs below this amount, and the landlord pays for repairs above it.
3. Can a landlord refuse to fix a major plumbing leak?
No, under RERA guidelines, the landlord is obligated to maintain the property in a habitable condition, which includes fixing major plumbing leaks and structural issues.
4. Do I need my landlord's permission to paint the walls?
Yes, you generally need written permission from your landlord to paint walls or make any significant alterations. You may also be required to return the walls to their original color before moving out.
5. What should I do if my landlord ignores my maintenance requests?
First, ensure all requests are in writing. If ignored, you can file a complaint with the Rental Disputes Center (RDC) at the Dubai Land Department.
