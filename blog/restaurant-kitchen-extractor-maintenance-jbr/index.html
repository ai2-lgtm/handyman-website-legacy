What Is Actually Happening Inside Your System

It is not just smoke you are extracting.

It is vaporized cooking oil. Tiny, aerosolized grease particles. Steam. Carbonized residue.

These particles are not all caught by the filters you see.

Many bypass them. They travel into the ductwork.

The ducts are cool. The grease condenses.

It coats every surface. A sticky, flammable film.

Layer by layer. Day by day. Hour by hour.

Especially in JBR, where humidity levels can fluctuate.

This humidity traps more airborne particles.

The system pulls in fine desert dust. This dust mixes with the grease.

It creates a thicker, more stubborn, highly combustible sludge.

Your kitchen generates an average of 450 liters of grease vapor per day.

A high-volume grill can push that to 700 liters.

Only 60% of that is captured by standard baffle filters.

The remaining 40% goes directly into your duct system.

It accumulates at bends. In vertical shafts. Around fan blades.

This is not just a cleaning problem. This is a physics problem.

The hot air from your stoves. The intense flames from your woks.

The temperature inside those ducts can exceed 100°C.

The auto-ignition temperature of cooking oil residue? As low as 360°C.

A small flame from a high-flaring pan. A spark from electrical wiring.

That grease-lined duct becomes a perfect fuel source.

A rapid fire channel.

It is not a matter of if. It is a matter of when.

This is the reality your kitchen faces every single shift.

Warning Signs You Cannot Ignore

Your kitchen extractor system is trying to tell you something.

Are you listening?

If you see visible grease accumulation on the hood's exterior or filters, it means the filters are oversaturated. They are no longer capturing grease effectively. The excess is now going straight into the ducts.

If you notice grease dripping from the hood onto cooking surfaces, it means the internal channels are completely saturated. The grease has nowhere else to go but back down. This is a critical fire hazard and a direct violation of hygiene standards.

If there is a persistent, stale cooking odor in the dining area or public spaces, it means your extraction is failing. The system is not removing enough cooking fumes. The air is recirculating, carrying the smell with it. This drives away customers in JBR and Dubai Marina.

If your kitchen is excessively hot or smoky during peak service, it means the airflow is severely restricted. The fan is struggling to pull air through clogged ducts. This indicates a significant blockage and a stressed system.

If you hear unusual rattling, grinding, or whining noises from the extractor fan, it means the motor is under strain. Grease buildup on the fan blades creates imbalance. Bearings can fail. This leads to costly breakdowns.

If your utility bills for electricity are higher than usual, it means the fan motor is working harder to compensate for reduced airflow. It is consuming more energy for less output. An inefficient, dirty system costs you more every hour it runs.

These are not minor inconveniences. These are red flags.

Each one points to a system that is failing.

A system that is becoming dangerous.

How Often / When to Clean Your Extractor

This is not a suggestion. This is a requirement.

Dubai Civil Defense mandates specific cleaning frequencies.

It depends on your kitchen's activity level.

Heavy-Use Kitchens: Every 3 Months.

If your restaurant operates 12-16 hours a day. High-volume grilling. Frying. Wok cooking. This applies to most busy establishments in JBR, Dubai Marina, or Palm Jumeirah. Your system accumulates grease rapidly. Every 90 days, you need a full deep clean. No exceptions.

Moderate-Use Kitchens: Every 6 Months.

If your kitchen operates 6-12 hours a day. Less intensive cooking methods. Or a smaller menu. This might apply to some cafes or smaller eateries in JVT or JVC. Still, significant grease buildup occurs. Twice a year is the minimum.

Light-Use Kitchens: Annually.

If your operations are minimal. Less than 6 hours a day. Limited frying. Mostly reheating or light prep. Some small coffee shops or bakeries. An annual deep clean is the bare minimum. But inspect monthly. Conditions in Dubai change fast.

Special Circumstances:

After a change in menu that involves more frying or grilling. After an incident that causes unusual smoke or grease production. Before a major inspection. Always clean before these events. Be proactive, not reactive.

The Dubai summer heat, reaching 45°C and higher, also accelerates grease hardening. This makes it tougher to remove. Plan your major cleans before June. Or schedule during slower periods like Ramadan. This also applies to residential kitchen extractor cleaning, though frequencies differ.

FAQ: Your Burning Questions Answered

How often should a restaurant kitchen extractor hood in JBR be cleaned?

For heavy-use kitchens (12-16 hours/day), cleaning is mandatory every 3 months. Moderate use (6-12 hours/day) requires cleaning every 6 months. Light use (2-6 hours/day) needs it annually. Dubai Municipality and Civil Defense regulations demand these schedules for fire safety.

What are the common signs my extractor system needs urgent maintenance?

Poor smoke extraction, visible grease accumulation on surfaces, unusual fan noises, increased kitchen temperature, and grease dripping from the hood are all critical indicators. Ignoring these signs means a fire risk is escalating rapidly.
