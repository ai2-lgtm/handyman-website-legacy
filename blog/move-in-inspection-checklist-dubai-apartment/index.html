Your Non-Negotiable Snagging Checklist for Dubai

Forget the quick glance. When you are taking possession of an apartment or villa in Dubai, you need to approach it like a forensic investigator. This is your insurance policy against future headaches and unjust security deposit claims. Here is the definitive checklist, broken down by category, that Handyman.ae recommends for every tenant.

1. Air Conditioning (AC) System – The Most Critical Check in Dubai

  • Power On & Temperature Test: Turn on every AC unit. Set it to the lowest temperature (e.g., 18°C) and the highest fan speed. Check the output temperature at the vent with a thermometer. It should be significantly colder than the ambient air, typically 10-15°C below room temperature within 10-15 minutes.
  • Noise Levels: Listen for unusual noises – rattling, grinding, or loud humming. A healthy AC unit should operate with a consistent fan sound.
  • Drainage Test: For split units, check the outdoor unit for water dripping from the drain pipe. Inside, check for any visible water stains around the indoor unit, indicating a blocked drain line.
  • Remote Functionality: Test all functions on the remote control (mode changes, fan speed, timer).
  • Filter Condition: Visually inspect the filters. They should be clean, not caked with dust. Dirty filters indicate neglect and impact performance.
  • Ventilation: Ensure all vents open and close properly and are not obstructed or damaged.

2. Plumbing & Water Systems – Leaks are Your Enemy

  • Water Pressure: Turn on all taps (sinks, showers, bathtubs) simultaneously. Observe water pressure. It should remain strong and consistent. Low pressure can indicate blockages or issues with the building's water supply.
  • Hot Water: Test both hot and cold water from all taps. Wait a few minutes for hot water to reach the furthest tap.
  • Drainage Speed: Fill sinks and bathtubs halfway, then pull the plug. Water should drain quickly and completely without gurgling or slow pooling. This is a crucial indicator of clogs.
  • Leak Detection:
    • Under Sinks: Open all cabinets under sinks (kitchen and bathrooms). Look for any signs of water stains, dampness, or active drips. Run the tap for several minutes while inspecting.
    • Toilets: Flush all toilets. Listen for continuous running water after the flush. Check the base of the toilet for leaks.
    • Showers/Bathtubs: Run showers and check for leaks from the showerhead, faucet, and around the base of the enclosure. Ensure no water escapes the shower area onto the bathroom floor.
  • Toilet Functionality: Check flush power, seat stability, and for any cracks in the porcelain.
  • Water Heater: If individual water heaters are present, ensure they are working and providing hot water.

3. Electrical Systems – Safety First

  • All Outlets: Bring a small device (phone charger, night light) and test every single electrical outlet in every room. Ensure they are live and firmly fixed to the wall.
  • Light Fixtures: Turn on every light switch. Ensure all bulbs are working. If a bulb is out, replace it to confirm the fixture itself is functional. Check for flickering or dimming.
  • Fans & Appliances: Test ceiling fans (if any), extractor fans in bathrooms and kitchen. Test built-in appliances (oven, hob, dishwasher, washing machine) for basic functionality – do they power on?
  • Distribution Board (DB): Locate the main electrical panel. Ensure it is easily accessible and clearly labeled. Do not open it unless you are qualified, but note its condition.
  • Switch Functionality: Ensure all switches operate smoothly and are not loose or damaged.

4. Doors & Windows – Security and Insulation

  • Opening & Closing: Open and close every door and window. They should operate smoothly without sticking, squeaking, or excessive force.
  • Locks & Latches: Test all locks and latches, including balcony doors and main entrance. Ensure keys work for all locks provided.
  • Seals: Inspect weather stripping and seals around windows and doors. Gaps can lead to dust ingress and AC leakage, especially critical in areas like Al Furjan or DIP.
  • Glass Condition: Check all glass panes for cracks, chips, or condensation between double-glazed units.
  • Screens: If present, ensure window screens are intact and properly fitted.

5. Walls, Ceilings & Floors – The Aesthetic and Structural Integrity

  • Paint Condition: Look for chips, cracks, peeling, or significant stains. Note areas that appear to have been poorly patched or repainted.
  • Water Stains/Dampness: Critically examine ceilings, corners, and lower walls for any discoloration, bubbling paint, or damp patches. These are red flags for water leaks from the roof or neighboring units.
  • Cracks: Differentiate between minor hairline cracks (common with building settlement) and larger structural cracks. Document all.
  • Flooring: Inspect tiles, laminate, or carpet for damage, loose pieces, deep scratches, or stains. Ensure grouting is intact.
  • Skirting Boards: Check for damage, detachment from walls, or signs of water swelling.

6. Kitchen & Bathrooms – High-Use Areas

  • Cabinets & Drawers: Open and close all cabinets and drawers. Check hinges, handles, and internal condition for water damage or significant wear.
  • Countertops: Inspect for chips, cracks, deep scratches, or burn marks.
  • Appliances (if included): Beyond basic power, check specific functions for oven (heating elements), hob (all burners), dishwasher (cycle start), fridge (cooling).
  • Tiling & Grout: Look for missing, cracked, or discolored grout in showers and around sinks.
  • Fixtures: Check towel rails, toilet roll holders, and mirrors for secure fitting and damage.

7. General & Safety – The Overlooked Details

  • Fire Alarms/Smoke Detectors: Locate and press the test button on all detectors. They should emit a loud alarm.
  • Intercom/Doorbell: Test for functionality.
  • Curtain Rods/Blinds: Ensure they are securely installed and functional.
  • Pest Control: Look for signs of pests (droppings, nests, dead insects). This is crucial in areas like Jumeirah Park or Emirates Hills where villas can be prone.
  • Keys: Confirm you have all necessary keys for the apartment, mailbox, and any common areas (pool, gym).

Every single item on this list must be systematically checked. Do not rush. Take your time. This is your property for the next 12 months, and its condition directly impacts your peace of mind and your wallet.

Why These Checks Prevent Future Headaches

Understanding *why* these checks are important goes beyond just finding a defect. It is about understanding the underlying mechanisms that lead to costly repairs and deposit disputes. Every item on our checklist is designed to expose a potential failure point, a weak link in the chain that Dubai's environment will inevitably exploit.

Take the AC temperature test. If the air coming out of your vent is not 10-15°C below ambient, it is not just "weak." It means the refrigerant charge is low, there is a blockage in the coil, or the compressor is failing. A low refrigerant charge indicates a leak in the system, which will only worsen, leading to a complete breakdown and potentially damaging the compressor. That breakdown will occur during the peak summer months, when demand for AC technicians is highest, and costs are at their premium. You do not want to be that tenant in The Lakes trying to get an emergency AC repair in August.

Similarly, slow drainage. This is not merely an inconvenience. It indicates a partial clog, often from accumulated hair, soap scum, or even construction debris left in the pipes. If left untreated, this partial clog will become a full blockage. When that happens, water backs up, potentially overflowing and causing water damage to your flooring, cabinetry, and even the unit below. And guess who gets blamed for the water damage? The tenant, unless they can prove the drainage issue was pre-existing and documented.

The electrical outlet test might seem minor. But a dead outlet could mean a tripped breaker, a loose wire, or even a deeper wiring fault. Loose wiring is a fire hazard. Faulty breakers can lead to intermittent power issues and appliance damage. Checking every single outlet ensures the circuit integrity and exposes potential safety risks before you plug in your expensive electronics.

The mechanism here is simple: early detection prevents escalation. A small drip today becomes a major flood tomorrow. A minor AC issue becomes a complete system failure. Documenting these initial conditions creates a transparent baseline, shifting the burden of proof away from you, the tenant, and onto the landlord for pre-existing issues. It is about mitigating risk, both for your comfort and your finances.

Dubai's Climate and Your Apartment's Longevity

Dubai is a beautiful, dynamic city, but it is also a harsh environment for residential properties. The extreme heat, high humidity, and pervasive dust mean that maintenance issues accelerate at a rate far beyond what you might experience in a temperate climate. This context makes a meticulous move-in inspection not just advisable, but absolutely essential, especially in high-rise communities like Dubai Marina, JBR, and Dubai Internet City, or the expansive villa communities of Jumeirah Park and Emirates Hills.

Consider the AC. In August, outdoor temperatures can hit 50°C, and surface temperatures on rooftops or balcony slabs can exceed 70°C. Your AC unit, whether it is a central chiller system or a split unit condenser, works overtime for 8-9 months of the year. This relentless demand puts immense strain on compressors, coils, and refrigerant lines. A unit that performed 'adequately' in April will likely fail catastrophically in July if it has underlying issues. Any pre-existing AC problem you inherit will become *your* emergency during the peak heat. Many newer buildings in JVC and Al Furjan, especially those constructed in the last 5-8 years, are now reaching an age where routine component failures are becoming more common. If your unit is 5+ years old, a thorough AC check is paramount.

Then there is the dust. Fine desert dust permeates everything. It clogs AC filters, settling on condenser coils, reducing efficiency and increasing electricity consumption. It gets into window and door tracks, causing friction and wear on seals. This constant abrasion contributes to deterioration faster than in cleaner environments. A loose window seal in a Palm Jumeirah apartment means constant dust ingress, increased AC load, and potentially higher DEWA bills.

Humidity is another silent killer. Coupled with fluctuating temperatures, it is a prime accelerant for mold growth, especially in poorly ventilated bathrooms or behind walls where leaks might be present. A small, unaddressed leak in a bathroom in Dubai Media City can quickly lead to widespread mold, damaging gypsum and paint, and creating health hazards. The humidity also impacts wooden elements like kitchen cabinetry and door frames, causing swelling and warping if there is any underlying moisture. This is particularly relevant for older villas in The Lakes or Springs where plumbing systems might be showing their age.

Finally, the constant settling and shifting of large, modern structures, combined with the occasional tremors, can lead to minor structural cracks and pipe joint stresses. What looks like a hairline crack in the wall of your Dubai Harbour apartment today could indicate a deeper, more significant issue that will expand over your tenancy. These are not just cosmetic. They are indicators of potential future problems that will impact your living experience and your wallet.

Frequently Asked Questions About Move-In Inspections

What is a move-in inspection and why is it important in Dubai?

A move-in inspection, or 'snagging,' is a detailed review of your new apartment before you accept possession. In Dubai, it's critical because the high heat and humidity accelerate wear and tear, and identifying pre-existing issues protects your security deposit from unjustified deductions when you move out. It establishes a baseline condition of the property.

How long do I have to report defects after moving in?

While immediate reporting is best, most tenancy contracts in Dubai allow for a grace period, typically 3 to 7 days, to report any hidden defects not immediately visible during the initial walkthrough. Always check your specific contract terms, but act swiftly to document and communicate any issues to your landlord or agent.

Should I hire a professional for a move-in inspection?

For significant investments like a new home in Dubai Marina or Jumeirah Park, a professional inspection is highly recommended. Experts can identify hidden structural, electrical, or plumbing issues that a layperson might miss, providing a comprehensive report that holds more weight with landlords. This reduces future disputes and unexpected repair costs.

What are the most common issues found during move-in inspections in Dubai apartments?

The most frequent problems we encounter include non-functional AC units or poor cooling performance, leaking sanitaryware, clogged drains, faulty electrical outlets, damaged kitchen cabinetry, and paint defects. Water damage, often hidden behind walls, is a recurring major concern due to Dubai's climate and building settling.

What documentation should I keep from the move-in inspection?

You must keep a signed copy of the inspection report, photographic or video evidence of all identified defects, and all correspondence with your landlord or agent regarding these issues. This creates an undeniable record should any disputes arise over your security deposit at the end of your tenancy.
