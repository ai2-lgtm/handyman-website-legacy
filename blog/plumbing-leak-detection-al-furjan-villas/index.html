Warning Signs

You need to be a detective in your own home.

These are the subtle clues a hidden leak leaves behind.

Do not ignore them.

Rising Water Bill: This is the most common and often the first indicator. If your DEWA water consumption suddenly jumps by 20% or more without a change in usage habits, you have a leak. Period.

Damp Spots or Stains: Look for discolored areas on walls, ceilings, or floors. Yellowish, brownish, or dark patches. These indicate water saturation. In a two-story villa, a stain on the ground floor ceiling often points to a leak in the bathroom directly above it.

Musty Odor: A persistent damp, earthy, or moldy smell, especially in areas like bathrooms, kitchens, or laundry rooms, is a strong sign. Mold thrives in hidden, moist environments.

Low Water Pressure: If one or more faucets suddenly have significantly reduced water pressure, it could mean water is escaping somewhere else in the pipe system.

Sound of Running Water: Listen when all taps are off. Do you hear a faint dripping or trickling sound coming from inside a wall or under the floor? It is not your imagination.

Cracked or Lifting Tiles: Water under floor tiles can cause them to loosen, crack, or even lift due to moisture absorption and pressure buildup. This is common in bathrooms and kitchens in JVT and Jumeirah Islands.

Peeling Paint or Wallpaper: Moisture behind a wall will cause paint to bubble, blister, or peel. Wallpaper will start to detach from the wall surface.

Warm Spots on Floors: If you have a hot water pipe leak under a concrete slab, the floor above it might feel noticeably warm to the touch. This is a critical sign for slab leaks.

Water Meter Test: This is a definitive DIY check. Turn off all water-consuming appliances and taps in your Al Furjan villa. Note the reading on your DEWA water meter. Wait 30-60 minutes without using any water. Check the meter again. If the numbers have changed, even slightly, you have a leak.

If you observe any of these signs, do not delay. Every hour a leak goes unchecked, the damage compounds.

Step 1: Initial Assessment and DEWA Meter Check

Our team arrives with a full suite of tools. First, we confirm the presence of a hidden leak. We conduct the definitive water meter test. We turn off all water sources. Monitor the meter for 30 minutes. If it moves, the hunt is on.

We also inspect visible areas: under sinks, behind toilets, water heater connections. We are looking for obvious culprits first. But typically, the obvious ones are already ruled out by you.

Step 2: Thermal Imaging (Infrared Cameras)

This is where the magic begins. We use high-resolution thermal cameras. These cameras detect minute temperature differences on surfaces.

Water evaporating from a hidden leak cools the surrounding area. Or a hot water leak warms it.

The camera translates these invisible temperature changes into a visual spectrum.

We can see the "ghost" of the leak behind plaster, tiles, and even some flooring. No demolition required at this stage.

This is crucial for identifying leaks in concrete slabs or within wall cavities without tearing anything open.

Step 3: Acoustic Listening Devices

Water escaping under pressure makes a sound.

A faint hiss. A drip. A gurgle.

Our highly sensitive acoustic listening devices can amplify these sounds.

We use ground microphones and listening rods. We sweep them across floors and walls.

The device visualizes the sound waves. The closer we get to the leak, the higher the frequency and intensity.

This allows us to narrow down the leak location to a specific square inch, even under several feet of concrete.

Imagine finding a pinhole leak under a meter of reinforced concrete in Dubai Marina. This tool makes it possible.

Step 4: Tracer Gas Detection

For the most elusive leaks, especially in complex pipe networks or under thick slabs, we use tracer gas.

We introduce a safe, non-toxic, non-flammable gas (typically a mixture of hydrogen and nitrogen) into the empty pipe system.

This gas is lighter than air and can penetrate almost any material – concrete, soil, tiles.

It escapes from the smallest leak point.

Then, we use a highly sensitive gas detector to sniff out where the gas is surfacing. This gives us an exact pinpoint location.

It is incredibly precise. We use this technique often in properties in Jumeirah Golf Estate where pipe networks are extensive and complex.

Step 5: Endoscopic Camera Inspection

If we suspect a leak inside a drainpipe or a cavity, we deploy a flexible, waterproof borescope camera.

This small camera, with its own light source, snakes through pipes or small access points.

We get a live visual feed. We see the crack. The separated joint. The intrusion.

This confirms the leak's exact nature and extent before any repair work begins.

Step 6: Detailed Report and Repair Plan

Once the leak is precisely located and identified, we provide a detailed report.

This includes thermal images, acoustic readings, and camera footage. Evidence.

How often should I check for leaks in my Al Furjan villa?

Perform a visual check monthly. Get a professional leak detection service every 12-18 months. Especially critical before peak summer or after any major plumbing work.

Can I detect hidden leaks myself without professional tools?
