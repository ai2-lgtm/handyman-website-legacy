Fair Wear and Tear vs. Damage: Decoding the RERA Grey Area

Dubai's Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) outlines general guidelines for tenancy, but the interpretation of "fair wear and tear" often becomes a contentious point between tenants and landlords. In essence, fair wear and tear refers to the natural deterioration of a property over time due to normal usage. Think of it as the inevitable effects of living in a space.

  • Fair Wear and Tear (Generally Accepted):
    • Slight fading of paint due to sunlight exposure.
    • Minor, almost imperceptible scuffs that can be wiped clean.
    • A few small nail holes (e.g., 1-2mm) from hanging pictures, provided they are not excessive or structural.
    • Minor discolouration behind furniture that has been in place for years.
  • Actual Damage (Often Leads to Deposit Deduction):
    • Large holes, cracks, or gouges in walls.
    • Extensive crayon, marker, or ink stains that cannot be easily removed.
    • Significant water stains or mould growth, especially if linked to tenant negligence (e.g., poor ventilation in bathrooms).
    • Chipped or peeling paint that goes beyond simple age-related fading.
    • Stains from spills, food, or pet accidents.
    • Repainting in a different colour without landlord's consent.

The challenge is that many tenancy contracts in Dubai explicitly state that the property must be returned in the "same condition" it was received, often interpreted by landlords as requiring a fresh coat of paint. This is particularly common in residential buildings in areas like JVC and Discovery Gardens, where properties see high tenant turnover. Property managers and landlords know that a pristine, freshly painted unit attracts new tenants faster and commands better rental value. Therefore, any perceived flaw on the walls, no matter how minor, becomes a leverage point for deposit retention.

Common Wall Damage Nightmares & Their Fixes

Let's delve into the specific wall issues that frequently trip up tenants and how they can be professionally addressed.

Nail Holes and Picture Hooks

The Problem: You wanted to make your JVC apartment feel like home, so you hung some artwork. Now, at move-out, the walls resemble a dartboard target. While small pinholes might be considered fair wear and tear, a multitude of larger nail holes or screw holes for heavier frames will not be.

The Technical Fix: These are usually simple repairs for a skilled painter. Small holes are filled with spackle or lightweight joint compound, sanded smooth, and then spot-primed before being painted over. For larger holes, a backing material or mesh patch might be required before filling to ensure structural integrity and prevent cracking. The key is precise colour matching and seamless blending with the existing wall texture.

Scuffs and Surface Marks

The Problem: The constant movement of furniture, children playing, or even just daily life in a bustling community like Discovery Gardens can leave unsightly scuffs and marks on walls. These range from light rub marks to dark streaks from shoes or bags.

The Technical Fix: The approach depends on the mark's severity. Light scuffs can often be cleaned with a mild detergent solution or a magic eraser. However, if the scuff has penetrated the paint layer or left a dark residue, cleaning can sometimes damage the paint further or leave a noticeable 'clean patch'. Professional painters will assess if cleaning is sufficient or if a light sanding, spot priming, and repainting are necessary. They understand paint types and how they react to cleaning agents, preventing further damage.

Crayon and Ink Art: The Tiny Picasso Effect

The Problem: Every parent in Dubai knows the horror of discovering a masterpiece drawn on a pristine wall. Whether it's crayon, marker, or even pen, these artistic expressions are rarely appreciated by landlords.

The Technical Fix: These marks are particularly challenging because they often bleed through standard paint. Professional painters use specialized stain-blocking primers (e.g., shellac-based or oil-based primers) that encapsulate the stain, preventing it from showing through the fresh topcoat. Without this crucial step, the crayon or ink will inevitably reappear, even after multiple coats of paint. This is a common oversight by DIY tenants.

Moisture and Mould Issues

The Problem: Dubai's humidity can be a silent enemy. Poor ventilation in bathrooms, persistent small leaks, or even condensation can lead to moisture patches and, eventually, mould growth. This is particularly prevalent in compact apartments found in areas like JVC, where airflow might be limited.

The Technical Fix: This is more than just a cosmetic issue. Mould indicates a moisture problem that needs to be addressed. A professional will first identify and rectify the source of moisture (e.g., sealing a leaking pipe, improving ventilation). Then, the mould must be properly cleaned and treated with fungicidal washes to kill spores. Simply painting over mould will only hide it temporarily; it will return and can pose health risks. After treatment, the area is primed with a mould-resistant primer and then repainted.

Chipped Paint and Peeling Sections

The Problem: Whether from impact, poor original paint adhesion, or general wear, chipped and peeling paint makes a wall look neglected and significantly impacts the property's aesthetic appeal.

The Technical Fix: This repair requires careful preparation. Loose or peeling paint must be scraped away completely. The edges of the remaining paint need to be feathered with sanding to create a smooth transition. Any imperfections are then filled, sanded, primed, and finally painted. Without proper surface preparation, new paint will simply peel off again, or the imperfections will show through.

Do I need to repaint my apartment before moving out in Dubai?

Generally, yes. Most tenancy contracts in Dubai stipulate that the property must be returned in the same condition it was received, 'fair wear and tear' excepted. This often includes a fresh coat of paint, especially if there are noticeable scuffs, marks, or colour changes. Always refer to your specific tenancy agreement. Handyman.ae can help you meet these requirements efficiently.

What is considered 'fair wear and tear' for walls in Dubai?

Fair wear and tear refers to the natural deterioration of a property over time due to normal use. For walls, this might include slight fading, minor scuffs that can be easily wiped away, or very small, isolated nail holes from hanging pictures. It generally does not cover large holes, crayon marks, significant discolouration, mould growth due to tenant negligence, or extensive chipping. The distinction can be subjective, which is why professional assessment and repair are often prudent.
