Why Exhaust and Grease Trap Maintenance Is Non-Negotiable

A commercial kitchen exhaust system pulls hot, grease-laden air out of the cooking line all day. Over weeks, that grease coats the inside of the ductwork and hood filters. This is not a cosmetic issue. It is a documented fire hazard and a compliance requirement.

Grease traps have a separate but equally important job: catching fats, oils, and grease before they enter the drainage system. A neglected trap backs up, smells, and can trigger a drainage violation during an inspection.

How Often a Commercial Kitchen Actually Needs Cleaning

Cleaning frequency depends on cooking volume and method. High-volume kitchens with grills and fryers running daily need exhaust hood and duct cleaning roughly every one to two months. Lower-volume kitchens can often extend to quarterly.

Grease traps follow a similar rhythm: busy restaurant kitchens typically need a trap pump-out every four to eight weeks. Waiting until the trap overflows means dealing with a drainage backup during service hours instead of on a scheduled visit.

The Fire Risk Hiding in an Unclean Exhaust Duct

Grease is flammable. A hood fire that starts at the cooking surface can travel straight up a grease-coated duct and spread far faster than staff can react. This is why exhaust cleaning is treated as a fire safety measure, not just hygiene.

A clean duct system also keeps exhaust fans running efficiently, reducing strain on the motor and cutting the risk of a mid-service breakdown that fills the kitchen with smoke and heat.

What Dubai Compliance Checks Look For

Inspectors typically check for visible grease accumulation inside the hood and duct, the condition and cleanliness of grease trap, and whether the business can produce maintenance records showing regular cleaning dates.

Restaurants without documented cleaning schedules are far more likely to face repeat visits or corrective notices. A dated service log from a professional provider is the simplest way to demonstrate compliance on the spot.
