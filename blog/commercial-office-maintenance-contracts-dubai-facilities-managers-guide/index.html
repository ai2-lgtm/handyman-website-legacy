What an Office Maintenance Contract Should Actually Cover

A real office maintenance contract in Dubai covers four things: AC systems, electrical, plumbing, and general carpentry or fixture repair. If your current contract only mentions AC, you are one broken washroom tap away from calling a second vendor mid-crisis.

Facilities managers should insist on a written scope document. It should list exactly which systems are included, how many preventive visits happen per year, and what counts as an emergency versus a scheduled repair. Vague scope language is how contracts fail during an actual outage.

Response Time Guarantees That Matter

The single most important line in any commercial contract is the response time guarantee. For a functioning Dubai office, that means a technician on-site within hours for anything that stops work: AC failure in summer, an electrical trip affecting a full floor, or a burst pipe near server equipment.

Ask your vendor to put the response window in writing, not just in a sales call. "We're usually fast" is not a guarantee. A named number of hours, tied to a penalty or credit if missed, is.

Why One Vendor Beats Five Separate Contractors

Many Dubai offices still run on a patchwork: one company for AC, another for electrical, a freelancer for plumbing. Every additional vendor is another phone call during an emergency, another invoice to reconcile, and another gap where no one takes ownership of a problem that touches two systems at once.

A single accountable vendor removes that gap. When an AC leak damages a ceiling and needs both AC repair and drywall patching, one team handles both instead of two vendors pointing at each other.

Red Flags in a Maintenance Proposal

Watch for proposals with no named response time, no list of included systems, and no single point of contact. These are signs the vendor will be hard to reach when something actually breaks.

Also watch for contracts that only offer reactive repairs with no preventive visits included. Preventive maintenance is what keeps an AC compressor from failing during a July heatwave in the first place.
