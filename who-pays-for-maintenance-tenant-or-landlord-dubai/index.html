Who Pays for Maintenance — Tenant or Landlord — in Dubai

TL;DR: In Dubai, routine maintenance costs for wear-and-tear and minor repairs under the Ejari tenancy contract are typically the tenant's responsibility, while major maintenance and MEP system repairs fall to the landlord. The Dubai Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) enforces this split, with a threshold usually around minor versus major repairs, ensuring clarity on who pays what in residential and commercial properties.

Dubai’s Ejari contract and RDC guidelines clarify that tenants cover minor repairs and wear-and-tear, whereas landlords handle major maintenance and MEP system issues, ensuring a balanced cost distribution in tenancy agreements.

The Direct Answer

In Dubai, the cost of maintenance is split between tenant and landlord according to the Ejari contract and Dubai Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) guidelines. Tenants are responsible for minor repairs and routine maintenance arising from normal wear-and-tear, while landlords cover major repairs including Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing (MEP) system faults and structural issues. This division ensures clarity and protects both parties in residential and commercial leases.

A common follow-up question concerns the exact threshold between minor and major repairs, especially in MEP systems like FAHU, FCU, or electrical boards. Generally, minor repairs such as replacing light bulbs or fixing leaking taps fall to tenants, whereas replacement or repair of major equipment requires landlord intervention. The RDC dispute resolution pathway supports this framework, providing a clear process if disagreements arise about responsibility.

The Full Answer With Dubai Context

Dubai’s rental regulations, governed by RERA, Ejari, and enforced by the Dubai Rental Disputes Centre (RDC), clearly define maintenance responsibilities. Tenants must handle minor repairs and routine upkeep caused by normal usage and wear-and-tear, such as fixing taps, light fittings, or patching small wall cracks. Landlords are obligated to manage major structural repairs, electrical board (MCB/RCD) replacements, plumbing system failures, and MEP equipment like FAHU or FCU units. This division aligns with Dubai Municipality and Civil Defence codes, ensuring compliance with safety and habitability standards.

Edge cases arise depending on property type and usage. For apartments under Owners Associations (OA), maintenance of common areas and shared MEP systems typically falls under the landlord or OA’s responsibility. Villas may have higher tenant responsibility for garden irrigation systems but not for core MEP components. Commercial leases often include specific clauses for maintenance cost allocation, sometimes with annual maintenance contracts (AMCs) with Empower or Emicool for cooling systems. Understanding these nuances is critical for avoiding dispute in Dubai’s hot, humid climate with Shamal winds and salt humidity accelerating wear.

What Goes Wrong When This Question Is Answered Badly

The Obvious Mistake

Misunderstanding who pays for maintenance leads to disputes between tenant and landlord, often resulting in delayed repairs and deteriorating property conditions. Tenants may refuse minor repairs believing they are the landlord’s responsibility, while landlords might neglect critical MEP repairs assuming tenants will handle them. This confusion compromises building safety, comfort, and legal compliance under Dubai’s regulatory framework.

The Hidden Mistake

Ignoring the Ejari split and RDC guidelines can cause hidden costs and legal exposure. For example, tenants might perform unauthorized repairs or replacements, voiding warranties or violating Civil Defence codes. Landlords who delay major maintenance risk penalties from Dubai Municipality or RERA, and increased insurance premiums. These hidden issues often surface later as costly disputes or regulatory non-compliance.

What Fails Later

Improper allocation of maintenance responsibility creates future problems such as accelerated MEP system failures in cooling plants, electrical faults triggering fire hazards per UAE Fire & Life Safety Code, or water damage from neglected plumbing leaks. Over time, unresolved wear-and-tear leads to depreciation reducing property value and complicating Ejari contract renewals or Ejari cancellations in Dubai’s competitive rental market.

Insurance / OA / Deposit / Legal Exposure

Failure to clearly assign maintenance costs exposes both tenant and landlord to insurance claim denials, OA fines, or deposit disputes at tenancy end. Without adhering to RDC rulings, legal exposure increases with potential enforcement actions by Dubai Courts or RERA. Mismanaged maintenance responsibility can also affect compliance with HACCP standards in commercial kitchens or DHA requirements in healthcare-related properties.

How This Actually Works in a Dubai Building

Dubai buildings follow strict MEP and regulatory frameworks that dictate maintenance responsibilities. For instance, the landlord must maintain major MEP infrastructure like the FAHU, FCU, chilled water systems managed by Empower or Emicool, and electrical distribution boards (MCB/RCD). Tenants handle minor plumbing fixes, light bulb replacements, and cosmetic wear-and-tear. These rules align with Dubai Municipality’s Building Permit conditions and Civil Defence inspections ensuring safety and operational integrity.

Dubai’s harsh climate with summer temperatures reaching 45°C, frequent Shamal winds, and coastal salt humidity accelerates wear on building fabric and systems. This necessitates clear maintenance roles to prevent premature equipment failure. For example, tenants living in coastal apartments must manage minor corrosion-related repairs internally, while landlords ensure major anti-corrosion treatments and system replacements. This split avoids excessive cost burdens and supports sustainable building management.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is responsible for fixing a leaking tap in my rented apartment in Dubai?

According to Dubai’s Ejari contract and RDC guidelines, fixing a leaking tap due to normal wear-and-tear is typically the tenant’s responsibility. This is considered a minor repair that falls within routine maintenance. However, if the leak results from a major plumbing fault or pipe burst, the landlord must arrange and pay for repairs, as it impacts structural integrity and building safety. Tenants should promptly report issues to avoid escalation and comply with Ejari terms.

What maintenance costs does a landlord cover under Dubai rental laws?

Landlords in Dubai are responsible for major repairs and maintenance including structural defects, electrical distribution board (MCB/RCD) faults, plumbing system failures, and MEP equipment such as FAHU/FCU units. They must ensure compliance with Dubai Municipality and Civil Defence codes, maintaining safety and habitability. Additionally, landlords often cover common area maintenance in buildings governed by Owners Associations (OA), including cooling plants managed by Empower or Emicool.

Can tenants refuse to pay for repairs they consider major in Dubai?

Tenants cannot refuse to pay for repairs clearly defined as their responsibility under the Ejari contract and Dubai Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) rulings. If a dispute arises about the repair’s classification, tenants and landlords can seek RDC mediation or adjudication. Tenants refusing to pay for legitimate minor repairs risk eviction or legal action, while landlords neglecting major repairs expose themselves to fines and enforcement by Dubai Municipality and RERA.

How does the Dubai Rental Disputes Centre (RDC) resolve maintenance payment disputes?

The RDC provides a structured dispute resolution process based on Ejari contract terms, Dubai rental laws, and regulatory codes. Both tenant and landlord present evidence including maintenance reports, invoices, and contract clauses. The RDC assesses whether the repair is minor or major, considering wear-and-tear and MEP system involvement. It then issues a binding decision assigning payment responsibility, which can be enforced through Dubai courts if necessary.

Are maintenance responsibilities different for villas compared to apartments in Dubai?

Yes, villas and apartments have different maintenance responsibility nuances in Dubai. Tenants of villas typically manage minor maintenance including garden irrigation and external fixtures, while landlords remain responsible for core MEP systems and structural repairs. In apartment buildings, common area maintenance and shared MEP infrastructure are usually covered by landlords or Owners Associations (OA). These differences reflect property type, usage, and Dubai Municipality regulations.

Does climate in Dubai affect maintenance cost responsibilities?

Dubai’s extreme climate with high heat, Shamal winds, and salt humidity accelerates wear on building systems, influencing maintenance needs. While responsibilities remain as per Ejari and RDC guidelines, both tenants and landlords must consider climate-related preventive maintenance. Landlords often invest in robust MEP systems and weatherproofing, while tenants should promptly address minor issues to prevent escalation. This shared approach helps manage accelerated deterioration unique to Dubai.
